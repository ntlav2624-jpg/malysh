\begin{abstract}
This paper presents a computational and theoretical framework addressing the Navier-Stokes blow-up singularity problem through a geometro-dynamic phase resonance approach. By introducing a Layer 7 resonant gating mechanism that dynamically modulates effective viscosity at critical energy thresholds, we successfully suppress unphysical velocity field divergences in numerical simulations. We demonstrate the stability of the proposed algorithm using lightweight execution environments, providing complete source code, visualization kits, and automated validation tools to ensure full scientific reproducibility.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The global regularity and existence of smooth solutions to the 3D incompressible Navier-Stokes equations remain among the fundamental open problems in mathematical physics. Finite-time singularities (blow-ups) represent a major theoretical hurdle in modeling extreme turbulent cascades and high-energy fluid dynamics. 

Instead of treating viscosity ($\nu$) as a strictly constant phenomenological parameter, we propose a shift: modeling viscosity as a phase-dependent dynamic variable controlled by local resonant frequency gates, designated here as the \textbf{Layer 7 architecture}.

\section{Mathematical Formulation}
We consider the modified incompressible Navier-Stokes equations coupled with a phase resonance feedback loop:

\begin{equation}
\partial_t \mathbf{u} + (\mathbf{u} \cdot \nabla)\mathbf{u} = -\nabla p + \nabla \cdot \left( \nu_{eff}(\mathbf{u}, x, t) \nabla \mathbf{u} \right) + \mathbf{f}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\nabla \cdot \mathbf{u} = 0
\end{equation}

where the effective viscosity $\nu_{eff}$ is dynamically modulated by the Layer 7 gating function:
\begin{equation}
\nu_{eff} = \nu_0 + \Theta \left( \|\omega\| - \omega_{crit} \right) \cdot \gamma \left( \int_\Omega |\nabla \mathbf{u}|^2 dV \right)
\end{equation}
